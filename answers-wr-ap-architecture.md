## Key risks to resolve before applying

1. **Q13: £150,000 minimum economic and financial standing.** Public sources suggest WR-AP has about three staff. Check the last filed accounts against the £150k threshold. Red Flag Alerts will verify the turnover figure, so it must be exact. If WR-AP falls short, Q14 (a guarantor) is the only route.
2. **Q15: insurance.** The form requires Employer's Liability of £10m, Public Liability of £5m and Professional Indemnity of £2m. ARB requires PI cover, but many small practices hold less than £2m. Get a quote to increase it, or answer "can commit to obtain before award".
3. **Q2(b): Constructionline Bronze.** This looks like a Hyde Housing Association requirement carried over into this form. It is still marked as required, so either register at Bronze or raise a clarification with ProcurePublic.
4. **Q18: one referee must be a direct public sector client.** Kingston Riverside (Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames) meets this.
5. **Policy uploads (Q17(b) to (f), Q21(d), Q29).** If any policy is missing, ProcurePublic's templates can fill the gap.

### 19 Sub-contractor management (draft)

WR-AP leads design teams of structural, M&E, sustainability, landscape and planning consultants. On Ham Close we worked with BPTW and the wider client team, and on Kingston Riverside we worked alongside the contractor, Murphy. We keep our supply chain healthy by:

- **Selection.** We appoint consultants for competence and fit, and check insurance, accreditations and capacity before appointment.
- **Clear scope.** We use written appointments with RIBA Plan of Work stage deliverables, so responsibilities are clear from the start.
- **Coordination.** We hold regular design team meetings with action logs and run a shared BIM/CDE environment, so information flows promptly.
- **Fair payment.** We pay sub-consultants within 30 days or less **[CONFIRM]** and flow down any client payment terms fairly.
- **Review.** We review sub-consultant performance at each stage and resolve issues early through direct conversation.

### 21 Health and safety arrangements (draft, under 500 words)

WR-AP's directors have overall responsibility for health and safety. Our signed H&S policy is reviewed annually and brought to the attention of all staff.

As designers, our main legal duty is under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. On every project we:

- **Eliminate, reduce or control foreseeable risks through design.** This covers construction, maintenance, cleaning and end use, including working at height, access for façade maintenance, fire safety strategy and the handling of heavy elements.
- **Record residual risks.** Our design risk register flows into the pre-construction information and the health and safety file.
- **Coordinate with the Principal Designer and other designers**, and confirm that clients understand their CDM duties.
- **Consider Building Safety Act 2022 requirements** where higher-risk buildings or the dutyholder regime apply. **[CONFIRM: competence declarations]**

Our own staff mainly face office and site-visit risks. These are controlled by:

- site-visit risk assessments and contractor inductions;
- appropriate PPE;
- lone-working procedures, with a check-in system;
- DSE assessments.

When we appoint sub-consultants, we check their competence and H&S arrangements first. We get competent H&S advice from **[CONFIRM: external H&S adviser]**. Accidents and near misses are recorded and reviewed, and we act on lessons learned.

### 26(b) Company summary (draft)

WR-AP is an architecture practice based in Teddington, south-west London, founded in 2018 by Sean Weston and Max Rengifo. We are on a mission to create rigorous, evocative and performance-led architecture: buildings and places that perform well for the people who use them, for the environment and over the long term.

Our work spans community and civic buildings, public realm, and residential new-build and retrofit. In Ham, we are designing the new community centre and a new home for Richmond MakerLabs as part of the Ham Close regeneration. That scheme will deliver 452 homes, 49% of them affordable, for Hill, Richmond Housing Partnership and Richmond Council. In Kingston, we won the competition to co-design Kingston Riverside, a key stretch of public realm, with the Royal Borough of Kingston, Creative Youth and Kingston First, supported by the GLA. Our residential work includes WR House in Kingston and the sustainable reinvention of 25 Coleshill Road in Richmond.

Engagement is central to how we work. Our approach to co-designing with young people and local groups has been recognised by Richmond Council. We offer architectural design from feasibility through to completion, planning and development advice, BIM, interior design and design technical advisory services. Our small, director-led team gives clients senior attention at every stage.
